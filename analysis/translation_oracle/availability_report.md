# #58 - translations as an interpretive oracle: acquisition report

Generated by `work/translation_oracle.py`.  Every row below is a probed candidate source; the probe evidence is pinned in `sources.tsv` and re-runnable with `--probe`.

## The headline

**The full Skobin parallel text could not be obtained lawfully, and that is recorded as a result, not a failure to finish.**  The Russian edition is a commercial 2006 АСТ/Адаптек printing (ISBN 5-17-037280-9 / 5-93827-069-3, 320 pp, series «Alternativa»); FantLab catalogues it and serves no text for it; and every full text reachable on the open web is an unauthorized mirror.  The ingestion gate refuses those eight hosts, and no body was ever requested from them - the mirrors were probed with HEAD only, to establish that they exist.

**The disambiguation signal was obtained anyway, from material that is lawfully quotable.**  Russian readers of the book publish quotations of the Skobin translation in their FantLab reviews.  12 such quotations are pinned here (short research quotations, attributed, the same convention `work/reference_corpora.py` applies to Gibson and Burroughs) and 11 of them resolve to a single English text inside the 225-page corpus.  One of the two left over is a one-word token, which cannot be unique by construction; see `alignment_report.md`.

**The attribution is what makes these quotable, so it is pinned to the source rather than typed.**  The twelve quotations come out of 3 reader reviews - josser (2011-05-10), Stirliz77 (2020-10-09), ekidonaa (2025-02-12) - each row of `fantlab_reviews.tsv` carrying the reviewer's account, `datePublished`, the `#responseNNNNNN` permalink and a SHA-256 over the block body, and each quotation's attribution is *derived* from that table.  An earlier revision typed the reviewer into each row and got six of the twelve wrong, crediting Reza Negarestani, whose name occurs on the work page only in the «Рецензии» bibliography as the author of an external review *of* the book.  `--verify-reviews` re-reads the page's own `schema.org/Review` markup and confirms every quotation still occurs in the review it names.

**The finding that matters:** the Russian edition preserves the book's symbol channel verbatim.  `=`, `::`, `//`, `++`, `/`, `<<`, `>>` come through untranslated, which makes the operator stream a translation-invariant key.  Two long quotations were located by the operator skeleton *alone*, with no lexical anchor at all, and both come out at **100% positional operator retention** against the English: 16/16 operators for one, 12/12 for the other.  RUQ01's 16-operator sequence occurs exactly once in the corpus's 10,145 operator tokens; RUQ02's 12-operator sequence occurs twice, and the two sites are byte-identical (pp. 5 and 221) - which is a finding about the book, not about the aligner.  That is the Rosetta stone this corpus actually has.

AER is `alignment_error_rate` in `work/translation_oracle.py`: the Gale & Church / Levow standard, counting deletions and insertions of null-collapsed links over the gold links of the same split.  It is a rate of errors per gold link, not a proportion of a fixed set, so it is **not bounded above by 1** - the proportional baseline sits near 2 because it proposes about as many wrong links as the gold has links.  The comparison that carries meaning is the gap between the two rows, not either value on its own.

## What was searched and not found

* the nettime list, named in the methods inventory as the third interview: the host is reachable but the `nettime-l` 2002 thread index carries no Siratori subject and the Wayback CDX index of nettime.org has no matching path.  Recorded as `searched_not_found`, not dropped.
* a lawful online edition: FantLab's reader endpoint 404s, the LibreBook entry has an empty chapter list, Litres and the NEL refuse the agent.
* an openly licensed EN-RU parallel corpus for aligner validation: not needed.  The aligner is scored against a built-in gold of 24 authored pairs with planted 1-n and n-1 operations, hermetic and offline, with the weights fitted on a tuning half and reported on a held-out half.

## Licensing position

No full text of the Skobin translation was downloaded, and no mirror's body was fetched.  What is used is (a) bibliographic records, (b) short attributed quotations of the translation inside third-party public reviews, and (c) the author's own published interviews.  If a licensed copy of the Russian edition is ever supplied to the repository, drop it in and rerun: the aligner and the entire oracle pipeline consume it unchanged, and the skeleton localiser below is the piece that would then align all 225 pages instead of eleven quotations.
